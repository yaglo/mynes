# GPU signal, receiver, and CRT model

[Gameplay showcase](https://yaglo.github.io/mynes-web/gallery/showcase/) · [4K gameplay and beam-height measurements](https://yaglo.github.io/mynes-web/gallery/closeups/)

This describes the SDL3 `mynes_gpu` frontend. The SDL2 composite renderer is a separate implementation. Frontend work is isolated from CPU/PPU accuracy changes.

## Signal source and calibration

The composite path starts with PPU colour/emphasis **codes**, not an RGB palette. They select the 2C02/2C07 voltage waveform. The cached 512 × 24 tables contain voltage samples. Normal playback uploads codes and generates that waveform on GPU; legacy source-edge diagnostic controls can select the equivalent CPU generator.

The NTSC table uses the published **75-ohm terminated measurements**, rather than scaling unloaded DAC levels with one emphasis factor:

| Rail, mV | Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 228 | 312 | 552 | 880 |
| High | 616 | 840 | 1100 | 1100 |
| Emphasized low | 192 | 256 | 448 | 712 |
| Emphasized high | 500 | 676 | 896 | 896 |

Blanking is 312 mV, reference white 1100 mV, and sync 48 mV. Internally, blanking is zero and the 788 mV blank-to-white span is one. Tests allow the source's approximately 10 mV noise plus 4 mV quantization uncertainty. E/F colour codes remain black under emphasis. PAL retains the measured 2C07 rails and alternating-line phase mapping. These measurements describe particular chips, not every console revision. [NESdev NTSC measurements](https://www.nesdev.org/wiki/NTSC_video), [PAL measurements](https://www.nesdev.org/wiki/PAL_video).

NTSC uses eight samples per dot; PAL uses ten, both at twelve samples per subcarrier cycle. A 341-dot line advances phase by four samples in NTSC and two in PAL. Gameplay phase comes from the existing PPU clock, including skipped dots. Synthetic patterns use the corresponding timing sequence. The receiver matrix starts neutral, without hidden saturation, temperature, or black-lift adjustments.

## Raster and receiver

The analogue buffers contain 341 dots × 262 NTSC or 312 PAL lines. Each buffer line starts at horizontal sync; active picture begins 65 dots later. Horizontal sync, porch, burst, borders and vertical blanking are generated before console/cable filtering. NTSC bottom-border/backdrop and monochrome porch-pulse behaviour are represented. PAL includes the top and side blanking. Vertical sync is a broad pulse on NTSC lines 245–247 and PAL lines 270–272. The PAL alignment is inferred from the PPU decoder transitions, including their one-line output delay. [BreakingNES PAL decoder](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emu-russia/breaks/master/BreakingNESWiki_DeepL/PPU/pal.md).

The main sequence is:

1. PPU DAC and full raster.
2. Console/cable filtering, RF impairments, echoes, and sync-keyed AGC.
3. Sync-edge and burst detection, receiver PLL, and back-porch clamp.
4. Y/C separation, luma filtering, quadrature chroma detection and filtering.
5. PAL delay-line correction when selected.
6. Colour matrix and independent RGB video amplifiers.
7. Video supply loading and incomplete DC-restoration recovery.
8. Deflection/convergence/focus, gun voltage-to-current conversion, and beam spread.
9. Recursive phosphor decay, physical mask coverage, glass/room effects, and output encoding.

Horizontal gates follow the detected sync edge instead of assuming the edge always arrives at its ideal position. A generic line loop smooths phase, clamp level, amplitude, and horizontal timing; it holds phase through missing bursts, suppresses colour during retrace, and reacquires afterwards. `h_pll_hz` replaces its horizontal part with a second-order PLL that keeps its state through vertical sync and raises its gain for 21 lines after it; the VHS preset uses 250 Hz. The sync slicer sits halfway up the sync pulse from its tip. Burst amplitude controls chroma gain and colour killing: the automatic chroma control holds the burst at the standard amplitude (40 IRE peak to peak of the 100 from blanking to white for NTSC, 300 mV of 700 for PAL) and the chroma follows. The 2C02's burst is a 47.7 IRE square wave whose fundamental is 60.7 IRE, so a receiver shows the console at two thirds of the chroma a decoder normalised to the console's own burst gives; the sky in Super Mario Bros. drives the blue phosphor to about white's level rather than 1.6 times it, and every colour comes out a third less saturated than before. AGC measures sync independently of picture brightness. The loop constants are behavioural values, not measurements of a named decoder IC. The vertical pulse is present in the signal, but a free-running vertical oscillator and loss-of-lock rolling are not simulated.

NTSC line combs retain a broadcast receiver's 2730-sample delay against the NES's 2728-sample line. A chroma BPF precedes the line operation, preserving low-band horizontal boundaries. Two-line mode uses one delay; three-line mode is centred on the middle of a two-delay store. Adaptive mode selects correlated neighbours and otherwise falls back to horizontal separation. These are generic topologies, not chip-exact algorithms. PAL bypasses NTSC combs and uses horizontal separation plus its chroma delay line. S-Video separates each code's cycle mean from its modulation at the source; Y carries sync and C carries burst. Filtering and echoes apply to both. This represents an ideal separated source, not a measured console modification. RGB similarly derives ideal DC/quadrature colour from each code's voltage cycle, skips reception, and enters at the RGB amplifiers. It still runs gun transfer, beam, convergence, load, phosphor and glass. Stock 2C02 has no RGB output; arcade RGB-PPU ROM palettes and particular RGB modifications are not emulated by this source option.

The NTSC composite/RF source now also has a voltage-dependent output-impedance stage. `console_phase_distortion_ns=30` selects the published 2C02G estimate; zero restores the ideal rails. It produces brightness-dependent hue rotation relative to burst, rather than an arbitrary palette adjustment. PAL and ideal separated/RGB sources do not apply this NTSC estimate.

The NES-001's own output stage is available as `console_follower_tau_ns` (Console menu, `Output follower RC`): from Schenk's schematic, Q1 pulls VIDEO_OUT down through the transistor but lets it rise only as R2 (510 Ω) charges C5 (330 pF) towards +5 V, 168 ns, so bright detail rises slowly and each palette row gets its own chroma gain and phase (about 5° per row across rows 0 to 2, the size of the NESdev 2C02G estimate). It is simulated in ngspice with the RF/AV module's traced video section in `tools/circuits/nes001_video_chain.cir`, and `gpu_fidelity_tests` holds the GPU stage to that run. Shipped presets keep it off (0) and the 30 ns estimate until compared with a console.

The source has a generic 6 MHz equivalent pole, separately adjustable from receiver bandwidth. Old presets that accidentally stored the audio 14 kHz corner are imported as 6 MHz. Cable filtering uses its shunt capacitance and the parallel source/termination resistance, instead of imposing an arbitrary 7 MHz limit on a short lead. This is a lumped approximation, not measured transmission-line loss. The old coupling-capacitor field was never implemented; it is retained only for file compatibility and removed from the OSD and shipped profiles. In particular, the former comment claiming 75 Ω / 10 µF was a 0.21 Hz no-op was wrong: it is about 212 Hz. No capacitor simulation is claimed.

RF uses negative-AM complex envelopes, complex Gaussian channel noise, a 97-tap asymmetric complex IF, then envelope detection. `carrier_level_dbm` is the sync-tip carrier reference; `noise_floor_dbm` is total noise power at the pre-filter injection point. Per-axis noise RMS is `sqrt(0.5) * 10^((noise-carrier)/20)`. Sync tip is unit carrier and reference white is 12.5%. Legacy zero values select −20/−70 dBm defaults. IF asymmetry and detuning are active controls; carrier frequency remains metadata. The generic IF is not a measured NES RF module, SAW filter or broadcast VSB transmitter. VHF oscillation and intercarrier audio are not simulated. The actual-decoder regression checks noise correlation at lags 1–12 independently of the colour cycle.

The optional VHS stage records the composite signal on an NTSC SP deck and plays it back before the TV receiver, in two compute stages per line. Record: keyed AGC, luma low-pass with colour trap, IEC pre-emphasis clipped at 160% white, FM deviation 3.4-4.4 MHz, colour-under band-pass. Tape: a per-line timing table read in playback time (per-head bows, field-varying error, line jitter and head-switch steps measured on a Panasonic PV-7450), FM modulation noise, head/tape response, white RF noise and dropouts with spacing loss. Playback: pulse-count discriminator, dropout compensator, de-emphasis, noise canceller, aperture, Y delay line, 1H chroma comb and up-conversion onto the fixed carrier, so timing moves the picture and leaves its hue unchanged. It applies only to NTSC composite/RF. [Model details, sources, measurements and omissions](architecture/gpu-realism-validation.md#vhs).

Component and RGB use the ideal source-modification path: integrate the DAC voltage cycle, decode colour and drive the complete CRT. Component no longer reads stale/zero composite chroma buffers. This is an ideal colour-difference roundtrip, without component cable/ADC losses; an unmodified NES has no component output. See the [complete preset audit](https://yaglo.github.io/mynes-web/gallery/presets/).

Backdrop outside the picture uses the core's frame-handoff palette/mask snapshot. Per-dot border palette writes and exact odd-frame raster pulse duration are not exported by this frontend. Core accuracy work remains separate.

## Beam loading, streaks, and breathing

The beam's dark and white spot widths are exposed as `beam_fwhm_min` / `beam_fwhm_max` in source scanlines. Gaussian pixel-area integration conserves energy through fractional resizing. Each gun's space-charge broadening follows its own current; shared supply focus is separate. Legacy sharpness/height fields are converted on import. Receiver noise is added to voltage before gun transfer and spatial spreading, not painted over drawable pixels. The Gaussian spot is still an approximation; measured high-current CRT spots can have non-Gaussian tails. [Infante, CRT spot analysis](https://sid.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1889/1.1984864).

Loading belongs to the receiver/CRT, after the RGB amplifiers. It no longer changes the console's transmitted composite waveform or forces a CPU waveform pass.

- `beam_current_load`: video-rail gain reduction from causal gun-current loading, with a nominal 12 µs recovery.
- `video_black_droop`: incomplete DC restoration. A causal luma-dependent bias produces a dark wake following bright material and a brighter wake following dark material. Zero disables it.
- `video_recovery_us`: bias recovery time; zero in older files selects 18 µs.
- `hv_sag`: raster-size response to local and regulated supply load. **Positive contracts, negative expands**, preserving the historical preset sign. Which direction dominates depends on the set's deflection and EHT regulation.
- `focus_breathing`: load-dependent focus growth, replacing a decorative sine-wave focus animation.

The shared supply follows successive scanlines with nominal 1 ms attack and 2 ms recovery, including discharge across vertical blanking. Local and shared loading affect deflection together. Skipped presentation frames advance this approximate supply using elapsed emulated time. A preset reset clears its supply memory. These time constants and couplings are a generic receiver model, not component measurements of the PVM. Overscan enlarges and crops the raster, while the visible tube aperture remains fixed; it no longer shrinks the picture into a border.

The tube face is the receiver's active raster, not the console's picture. The deflection stage places the 256×240 picture on it from the standard timings (ITU-R BT.470: NTSC 63.556 µs lines with 10.9 µs of blanking after a 1.5 µs front porch and 21 blanked lines of the 262; PAL 64 µs, 12 µs, 1.5 µs and 25 of 312) and the console's own signal, whose picture starts 65 dots after horizontal sync with the vertical sync 17 lines before the frame's end (22.5 for PAL). With a NES dot of 186.24 ns, the active NTSC line is 282.73 dots and the picture starts 14.53 dots in, so NES pixels are 8:7 on a 4:3 face and a set without overscan shows 5.1% of blanking on the left and 4.3% on the right; the picture's first line falls in the vertical blanking and 2 lines of blanking follow its last. PAL shows 3.3% and 4.2% either side and 19.5 lines above the picture. A preset's overscan then enlarges the raster over the face as the set would, hiding these edges on consumer sets.

Stas's Favourite deliberately retains visible horizontal recovery and modest contraction/focus change. Living Room, Bedroom RF and Famicom Kitchen retain milder supply behaviour. The reference and nominal PVM stay regulated. OSD and Signal Studio expose these controls, and preset save/load preserves them.

Video-amplifier supply ripple and coupling/DC-restoration faults can produce horizontal streaks; EHT/deflection regulation can change raster size and focus. The models represent these mechanisms without claiming that a screenshot identifies a particular failed component. [Repair FAQ: video-amplifier streaks](https://www.repairfaq.org/REPAIR/F_monfaq.html), [TI: coupling and line droop](https://e2e.ti.com/support/data-converters-group/data-converters/f/data-converters-forum/334435/tvp5150am1-clock-grounding-and-power-up-sequence-questions), [Repair FAQ: blooming](https://www.repairfaq.org/samnew/tvfaq/tvbloom.htm).

## Light, phosphors, and HDR

Gun voltage becomes linear emitted current **before either axis of spatial beam spread**. Horizontal blur therefore preserves the light from a narrow highlight rather than averaging voltage and then losing energy through gamma. Vertical Gaussian spots integrate over each output pixel's footprint. Changing focus spreads energy; it does not create it. Beam width follows current, including load-dependent focus and convergence. Tests cover isolated highlight energy, fractional scanline scaling, and narrow/wide spots.

Beam and history buffers are RGBA16F. Phosphor decay is recursive per channel and accounts for elapsed emulated frames. An optional second history has its own decay time and integrated-energy weight; both states preserve steady light. It is allocated only when enabled. Explicit display smoothing remains separate from receiver comb filtering and is disabled across dropped-picture gaps.

Presets specify a triad count across the tube. Host display settings separately choose **Panel pixels** (default) or **CRT pitch**. Panel mode fits each complete RGB triad and mask-row pitch to whole native panel pixels using the actual 4:3 game viewport. Its effective triad count therefore differs from the nominal tube at limited resolutions. CRT-pitch mode retains nominal density and filters unresolved detail. `F`/`F11` selects native fullscreen in panel mode. The OSD reports drawable size and effective density; window resize, movement and fullscreen recalculate mask coordinates.

**Panel subpixels** (M → Host display, `--panel-subpixels off|rgb|bgr`, saved as `gpu_panel_subpixels`) tells the renderer the panel's subpixel order. It is not detected: most LCDs, including MacBook panels, are RGB stripes; many OLEDs are not stripes at all and should stay Off. With an order set, each host channel shows the phosphor light at its own subpixel, a third of a pixel from the pixel centre, and an aperture grille is integrated over the one-pixel window between same-colour subpixels. Panel-pixel mode then allows triads down to one pixel instead of three: the PVM-14L2's 1070 triads across a 2219-pixel picture (2.07 pixels each) become two-pixel triads, 1110 across, where three-pixel triads gave 740. At a whole-pixel period each stripe moves onto the nearest subpixel of its colour, so a two-pixel triad lights one pixel's red, green and blue subpixels and leaves the next pixel dark. Concentrating a triad's light into half the pixels needs twice the output level; where the host has no headroom for that, the output shoulder compresses the stripes and keeps their colour ratios, while the gaps between stripes stay dark, as the grille's wires keep them on a tube. At a whole-pixel period, with or without subpixels, the grille is drawn as exact stripes: each colour gets the share of its stripes inside its sampling window, so pixels between a colour's stripes get none of it. Any other period would beat against the pixel grid, so it is band-limited instead, at each colour's subpixel when those are drawn. The setting applies only while the desktop is not resampled (native fullscreen or an unscaled window) and stays Off in offscreen, screenshot, recording and benchmark runs unless given on the command line. It costs about 0.7 ms per frame at 2560×1920 on an M5.

**Damper wires**: an aperture grille is held by one horizontal tungsten wire of 20 to 30 µm on tubes of about 15 inches and smaller and by two on larger tubes (US5369330; Sony CPD-220AS manual, two wires about 6 cm from the top and bottom of a 17-inch tube). The wire blocks the beam along its length and leaves a thin unlit band on the phosphor. Presets give `face_height_mm` (visible face height), `damper_wires` (0 to 2), `damper_y1` and `damper_y2` (height from the top as a fraction of the face) and `damper_wire_um`; the band is integrated over each output row, so a 25 µm wire on the PVM-14L2's 200.6 mm face darkens one row of a 1664-row picture by about 20%. Without a face height no wire is drawn.

Aperture stripes use a positive Fejer reconstruction and analytic footprint integration; unequal within-triad and between-triad gaps preserve a common vertical divider in white. Dot/slot masks use half-float periodic coverage and mip filtering. Dot rows shift horizontally; inline slot masks retain vertical RGB columns with alternating bridge positions. DC normalization keeps primary means neutral. Integer panel periods avoid a slowly beating uniform mask; beam/mask interaction and any later scaling can still introduce visible patterns. At insufficient resolution, matching the exact tube pitch and retaining individually resolved phosphors are incompatible requirements.

**RGB sources.** A preset whose connection is RGB is fed by a 2C03 RGB PPU: its palette is the documented 64-entry table with three bits per gun, as gun voltages in sevenths, and an emphasis bit drives its gun to full (NESdev, PPU palettes). The monitor's white drive, gun gains, contrast and brightness apply; hue and saturation do not, since there is no chroma. It used to derive RGB from the composite DAC voltages without the burst normalisation, which put a blue field at 2.8 times white. The 2C04's four permutations and the 2C05 are not represented.

`hdr_gain` (HDR emission gain) multiplies linear light in HDR output only; SDR output has no headroom to spend and uses 1. With **M → Host display → HDR gain: Auto** (the default, saved as `gpu_hdr_gain_mode`) the display measures the preset's own white instead of assuming it: when a preset is applied, the picture size changes or the headroom changes, 24 frames of NES white ($30) run through the preset's chain until its loops settle, the result goes through the display pass with the mask at gain 1 and no shoulder into a scratch target, and the 99.8th percentile of that field's pixels becomes the fit's peak (its stripe centres on line centres are a few per cent of the field; a preset's noise or dropouts must not set the gain) (the OSD's `GAIN` and the offscreen log's `white peak` show the result). The chain's temporal state is cleared afterwards so the white does not linger. The gain then puts that peak at 95% of the display's peak and starts the output shoulder there, so white keeps its line and stripe shape at nearly all the light the panel has. On a 2.0-headroom MacBook panel the PVM-14L2 measures a white peak of 3.97 and gets 0.48; a coarse dot-mask preset measures 1.6 and gets 1.2. Until the first measurement, and for the lab's own fit, the analytic estimate stands in: the scanline peak 1 + 2e^(−2π²σ²) and higher terms (σ the white beam FWHM over 2.355) times the mask's peak coverage at its strength, which for an aperture grille is exact and for dot and slot masks is read from the mipmapped tile; a test checks that estimate against drawn peaks to 1%. A CRT's white is dimmer than the panel's, so the gain is usually below 1; it is at most 4. Offscreen HDR captures measure the same way with the headroom they are given, so they stay reproducible. **Auto boost** (M → Host display, `gpu_hdr_boost`) multiplies the fitted gain by 1 to 2; above 1 the stripe centres pass the knee and their excess spills into the triad's other pixels, so the picture brightens in proportion and the grille softens in the brightest areas. The gain is at most 4. Offscreen HDR captures and recordings fit the headroom they are given (`MYNES_OFFSCREEN_HEADROOM`, `--record-headroom`), so they stay reproducible. The OSD status shows the gain in use. On macOS the window's layer is switched to extended linear Display P3 and the phosphor matrix is composed with the sRGB-to-P3 conversion, so each output channel drives the panel's own primary instead of being mixed by the compositor, and P22 colours outside sRGB are kept; the OSD panel line shows P3, **M → Host display → Panel primaries** switches back to sRGB for comparison, and files stay sRGB. Elsewhere HDR writes extended linear sRGB using SDL's current headroom and SDR-white scale; SDR applies the sRGB transfer function once at output. A continuous shoulder begins at 75% of available headroom (95% under Auto gain) and asymptotically approaches the host peak. It is driven by the brightest pixel of the triad, from the unmasked light and the mask's peak coverage. The light that pixel cannot show is not lost: it moves to the same colour's subpixel in the triad's other pixels, since the tube's 70 µm stripe is wider than the 30 µm subpixel it lands on. The triad's mean stays exact, so brightness follows the gain (Auto boost brightens a grille as much as a plain picture), colour keeps its hue, and the grille softens only where the drive exceeds the panel, the way a tube's spot blooms at high beam current. A per-pixel shoulder had squeezed a bright blue stripe on its own and turned its triad purple, and a triad-wide one had held the grille's brightness at the ceiling. This is explicitly display adaptation after tube physics: it preserves highlight structure better than independent channel clipping, but compresses the simulated peak-to-average ratio. Lower light output or more host headroom reduces that compromise. This is relative luminance, **not an absolute-nit calibration**. The current display's HDR/SDR mode and headroom appear in the OSD. `--sdr` permits an explicit comparison. Ambient black extends across window margins.

Glass scattering redistributes light with a nominal Gaussian sigma of 0.6% of picture height, independent of window resolution. Matte faceplate scatter filters the emitted, masked light at a radius tied to phosphor pitch. Horizontal beam weights are prepared once per dispatch instead of recomputing exponentials per sample. Normalized horizontal and vertical spots continue widening above reference-white current. Neither optimization changes the intended energy normalization.

 room reflection, phosphor grain, aging, thermal purity, and microphonic controls remain generic approximations. A static image cannot validate CRT impulse response. A normal sample-and-hold LCD still has different motion persistence, panel response, pixel structure, and peak luminance from a scanned CRT; ordinary 60 Hz playback does not reproduce the physical moving beam. No claim of indistinguishable hardware reproduction is made.

## Sony PVM-14L2 nominal profile

The Sony profile uses the published 267.5 × 200.6 mm viewable raster, 0.25 mm aperture-grille pitch (about **1070 triads across**), D65 option, and 10 MHz RGB bandwidth. It uses full phosphor-mask modulation. At limited resolution, the default panel mode fits a coarser grille; physical-pitch mode preserves nominal density while filtering it. Sony specifies a P22 tube and 600 TV lines; TV lines are not a triad count. [Sony specification](https://www.sony.jp/pro-monitor/products/PVM-14L2/), [Sony catalogue](https://www.sony.jp/products/catalog/SPC_PVM-20N6J.PDF).

Sony also specifies 120 cd/m² standard luminance. We do not turn that into an arbitrary HDR multiplier: physical output needs the LCD's measured white level. The composite preset now uses a generic adaptive comb with 0.75 MHz chroma half-bandwidth, following the documented MC141627/CXA2163 path. Receiver loop, gamma, detailed beam shape, phosphor spectra/decay, ABL and unit condition remain generic. The preset is explicitly named **Sony PVM-14L2 (nominal)** rather than claiming calibration to the user's unit.

The Zenith System 3 profile is a 19-inch table set of 1981 (L1960P9 class) on the NES RF modulator, channel 3. Zenith's 1984 catalogue gives the tube family (Chromasharp 90 and 100: an in-line gun, phosphor stripes, a black matrix), the Z-1 chassis, the PRP circuit's 330 lines and Color Sentry's tint stabiliser and contrast regulator; the L1960P9 owner's manual gives the controls. The profile takes a slot mask at a 0.78 mm stripe pitch (480 triads across a 375 mm face), 3.8 MHz luma with the sharpness control's peaking and no comb, 500 kHz chroma, a cool factory white at 9000 K and a beam that blooms with current. The pitch, bandwidths and spot are class estimates for a 90-degree in-line tube of the time, not measurements of a set; the archive.org items are `1984_Zenith_Catalog` and `zenithsystem3manual`.

## Playback and audio

A worker owns the core and audio clock; the main thread owns UI/presentation. A bounded three-picture FIFO preserves consecutive composite phases across brief UI/scheduling stalls. At matched-refresh Hold, a full queue waits for the display to consume a picture, preserving source-phase order. Other modes drop the oldest picture on sustained overload. Queue capacity is bounded in both cases; pause and shutdown wake a waiting worker. Swapchain waits and window/OSD work no longer directly pause the APU. Pause waits for a frame boundary, clears audio and stale pictures, and hands core ownership back before cartridge replacement. A paired offline capture can explicitly preserve both consecutive pictures.

The core supplies 44.1 kHz resampled mono samples with its analogue filters set to unity. This frontend applies console → cable → speaker processing once. The NES-001 console path comes from its schematic through ngspice (`tools/circuits/nes001_audio.cir`, with the 74HC04 gate as a MOS pair fitted to its data sheet): a 16.7 Hz high-pass from the 1 µF coupling against the mixing resistors and the gate's summing node, matching rainwarrior's measured 16 Hz; two low-pass poles, 15.4 kHz from the 47 kΩ/220 pF feedback and the 0.01 µF at the output pin on the gate's closed-loop output resistance, which the chain carries as 21.5 kHz; the gate's 3 V peak-to-peak rail window (`audio_gate_window_v`, Gate rail window in the Audio menu) at 2.0 V per mixer unit; and the 7805's ripple at twice mains from `nes001_psu.cir` through the gate's +9 dB supply gain (`audio_psu_hum_amplitude`, 0.00023 on the NES-001 presets). Mains pickup on the lead is `audio_pickup_mv` through what the cable's shielding lets past. The set's line input resistance is `audio_tv_input_kohm` (47 kΩ unless a manual says otherwise); on RF the cable slot becomes the set's 75 µs (50 µs PAL) sound de-emphasis, there being no pre-emphasis in the console's modulator, and the FM link's hiss follows the RF stage's carrier and noise levels with the sound carrier taken 13 dB under vision. The PVM-14L2 and Toshiba 14AF43 speaker profiles carry their amplifiers' output capacitors from the service manuals (199 Hz and 20 Hz); the drivers are class estimates. The Famicom's 37 Hz high-pass is its mixing network; its low-pass, the NES-101's and the Dendy's values are generic. The former 90 Hz and 440 Hz high-passes had no circuit behind them. CPU and GPU use the same continuous state, coefficient preparation and counts. GPU audio is the default, with one bounded in-flight block and a fence-polled readback. If its short deadline expires, CPU processing starts from the unchanged input state; the late GPU result is discarded. GPU initialization failure also selects CPU processing. `A` or **M → Audio → Processing** switches backend; `MYNES_GPU_AUDIO=0` selects CPU at startup. Backend selection is a session setting, independent of the picture preset.

The core now advances the PPU at the hardware master-clock ratios: 12:4 for NTSC and 16:5 for PAL. Audio uses the region's CPU clock, including PAL frame-sequencer, noise and DMC periods; the former workaround for a 3:1 PAL core has been removed. Frontend presentation pacing applies the corresponding wall-time resampling ratio without changing emulated CPU/APU cycles. Ten hardware-tested PAL APU ROMs pass in CTest.

SDL receives one mono block per console frame. Queue feedback uses **input** bytes and limits resampler correction to ±0.5%. The target is 10 ms before adding a frame; stale queued input above 80 ms is cleared with a short fade. These numbers exclude the device's output latency. Severe CPU contention can still underrun. Speaker responses and nonlinear processing at 44.1 kHz are generic, not measured cabinet models.

## Editor, captures, and validation

Signal Studio exposes active preset, modified state, save-as/save/rename/delete, and live model controls. Socket backpressure is bounded; a temporarily stalled reader must not disconnect or lose preset commands. Topology changes and telemetry have separate observations; timing is throttled to 4 Hz. Stage timings are **CPU command encoding**, not GPU execution measurements.

`F5` and `--screenshot-after N` capture the **final CRT display**, including mask, glass and margins. Each PPM has a `.linear.pfm` sidecar preserving relative linear HDR values. `--screenshot-pair` captures actual frames N and N+1, retaining both phases. `review_captures.py` averages those floats in linear light for a still exposure, then makes PNG overviews/crops. It does not enable a playback smoothing filter. SDR previews clip highlights above reference white. Interactive captures copy the readback into one owned CPU image for background encoding/writing; shutdown joins the writer and reports errors. Another capture waits for the previous writer, bounding memory. `--screenshot-after` and paired/sequence batch captures remain synchronous. Hidden capture reads the existing final target, avoiding a second glass/mask render.

For reproducible game captures, `MYNES_REVIEW_START_FRAME=N` presses Start for two emulated frames beginning at N. `MYNES_REVIEW_OSD=1` opens the setup menu. These diagnostics are opt-in. See the [visual review](https://yaglo.github.io/mynes-web/gallery/visual-review/) for paired captures and remaining limits.

The [hardware research](https://yaglo.github.io/mynes-web/research/hardware/) records sources and stage-by-stage assumptions. The image review script requires NumPy and Pillow. Its overviews resize linear light before sRGB encoding; `--exposure 0.5` preserves more EDR highlight detail in an SDR review. Native crops are not resampled. Tests cover voltage rails, CPU/GPU DAC equivalence, raster/retrace, delayed sync, burst dropout and reacquisition, AGC, PAL separation, causal bright/dark recovery, deflection sign/focus, beam energy, decay, mask/HDR output, asynchronous audio cancellation, playback pause/ROM replacement, and preset round trips. AccuracyCoin is outside this frontend validation.

`--benchmark` runs the actual selected preset from code upload through the final offscreen CRT pass at four fixed resolutions. Twelve warmups precede sixty individually fenced frames. It reports median, mean, p95 and maximum CPU-submission-to-GPU-completion time. Emulation/audio/vsync/capture are excluded; driver scheduling and host contention are included. Validation is off for performance runs and opt-in in playback with `MYNES_GPU_VALIDATION=1`. See [measured results](gpu-benchmark-results.md).

## Colour response and reproducible offscreen review

`crt_color.h` separates decoder colour-difference gain, voltage-domain white balance and emitted-light primaries. White temperature follows the CIE daylight locus (nominal 6500 anchors D65), preserving Y before solving for each gun's voltage gain. `phosphor_gamut` selects legacy 709, nominal 525/SMPTE-C or nominal 625/EBU primaries. The final matrix operates on linear phosphor light; P22 is not a claim of measured SMPTE-C chromaticities. [ITU-T H.273](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-H.273-201612-S%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items), [daylight white-point construction](https://docs.acescentral.com/white-point/).

Horizontal spot growth uses two normalized kernels weighted by each source gun's current, so widening deposits light rather than inventing it. The PVM preset's vertical FWHM runs from 0.28 line at low drive to 0.52 at full white, with a 1.6-sample horizontal spot that does not grow with current, the same focus as the Fine aperture grille preset. These values come from the maintainer's PVM, on which full-white scanlines stay separated and which looks closer to the Fine preset than to the earlier 0.38 to 0.90 profile, where adjacent white lines filled the gap between them to 82% of their peak. They are an observation, not a measured spot profile.

`--offscreen 2560x1664` renders into an exact-size hidden target and mutes SDL output while continuing audio processing and queueing. It uses fixed 1.6× review headroom, overridable with `MYNES_OFFSCREEN_HEADROOM` or, when recording, `--record-headroom`; `--record-hdr` defaults to 4.0 and renders into its own RGBA16F target. Offscreen, screenshot, recording and benchmark runs take turns: each waits for a lock on `/tmp/mynes_gpu-batch.lock` before it starts SDL, so a script that launches several runs gets them one at a time. `MYNES_BATCH_LOCK` names a different lock file, and `MYNES_BATCH_LOCK=0` turns the queue off. Offscreen runs start as background apps without a Dock icon. Playback tests default to this mode. Window presentation/vsync are necessarily absent; real core, APU, audio and the full GPU render remain included. `--screenshot-frames N` records up to 240 consecutive final renders. A `--preset` file is loaded by its actual path; compact JSON is supported and malformed files fail instead of silently producing a zeroed preset.

Static deflection maps are cached only when geometry has no enabled time, audio or image-load response. A parameter change, resize/allocation change or temporal reset forces recomputation. Stage snapshots remain available to Signal Studio while computation is reused. `benchmark_pipeline.py --recompute-geometry` provides a same-binary A/B comparison without changing the preset or normal playback. See the [controlled measurements](gpu-benchmark-results.md#geometry-optimization-controlled-comparison).
